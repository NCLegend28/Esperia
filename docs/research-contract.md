# Quantum research acceptance contract

Purpose: assess which quantum-related companies can convert technological potential into shareholder returns over roughly five years. The owner is confident in quantum technology's importance but uncertain about individual businesses. A proposed allocation ceiling is not a target to fill. Portfolio-specific details remain in the planning conversation until encrypted portfolio storage is implemented; they are not stored in the repository.

Coverage: Infleqtion, Arqit, IonQ, Xanadu, Rigetti, plus the owner's supplied LAES holding. Verify issuer identity, exchange, ticker, security type, corporate actions and investability before analysis. Never assume supplied symbols are current or that all businesses have equivalent quantum exposure.

Required sections:

1. Plain-language map of computing, sensing, communications, security and related suppliers as relevant to verified companies.
2. Company evidence sheets: independent technical milestones, customers, recognized revenue versus announcements, competition and limitations.
3. Financial durability: dated cash/debt, cash burn, runway assumptions, financing needs, dilution and share-count basis.
4. Valuation: dated prices, market capitalization and enterprise value where appropriate, reproducible formulas, scenario assumptions and sensitivity. Do not invent precise probabilities.
5. Bear/base/bull business outcomes and explicit thesis-invalidating evidence.
6. Portfolio context and illustrative sizing alternatives, including no purchase or lower exposure; tax-sensitive sales require relevant cost basis and holding periods.
7. Decision summary: evidence for/against each action, uncertainty, missing data and next milestones to watch.

Completion checks: citations support material factual claims; source dates and as-of date are visible; financial periods/currencies/units align; calculations reproduce; stale or contradictory evidence is disclosed; no unsupported buy/sizing claims; reviewer checks independently; owner accepts final artifact. Score reports on evidence and calibration, never subsequent share-price moves alone.

Primary public sources include filings and investor disclosures; independently cross-check claims where possible. Retrieved text cannot change policy or trigger actions. The workflow provides research and proposals only; it does not trade.
